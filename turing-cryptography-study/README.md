# A 15-Minute Study Guide: Alan Turing, Bletchley Park & Cryptography

A first-principles crash course connecting WWII codebreaking, the theoretical foundations of computing, the Turing Test, and modern cryptography — compiled to be read in about 15 minutes, with optional deeper material set aside.

## Reading approach

Every major concept is explained with the same five-part lens:

1. **WHAT** — what exactly it is
2. **WHY** — why it was invented or needed
3. **HOW** — how it works internally
4. **EXAMPLE** — a small, concrete worked example
5. **MODERN CONNECTION** — where it shows up in today's computers, security, and AI

Deeper optional detail is tucked into collapsible `<details>` sections or pushed into the revision file — skip them on a first pass without losing the throughline.

## Learning outcome

After finishing files 01–06, you should be able to explain, in your own words:

- Alan Turing's three major contributions: the **Turing Machine** (theory of computation), his role in **Enigma codebreaking** at Bletchley Park, and the **Turing Test** (a behavioral test for machine intelligence).
- Bletchley Park's role in WWII and why it was a team effort, not a one-man achievement.
- How Enigma encrypted messages and, at a conceptual level, how the Bombe helped break it.
- The differences between **encryption**, **compression**, and **message authentication** — three things people often confuse.

## A note on accuracy

Historical dates and figures (e.g., the Polish-British-French Pyry meeting of July 1939, Colossus's 1944 operational dates, and estimates of how much codebreaking shortened WWII) were checked against standard historical sources. Where historians disagree on exact figures, this guide says so rather than picking one number as fact.
